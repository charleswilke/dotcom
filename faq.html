So what do you actually do? ▼
The standard answer is I explore what it feels like to co-create with AI. There's a depth to that answer that might not be immediately evident. The feeling has been the journey, just as much as the technical evolution of my skills.

I'm in my 40's, I've had a whole career before AI making stuff. So the "you're not creative if you use this technology" just doesn't jive with my track record.

I chose to sacrifice the concept of my personal brand because my marketable skills routinely evaporate. My college education got eradicated when Snapchat delivered pixel tracking to the masses. Media production democratized long before ChatGPT. I've endured wave after wave of disruption and displacement. Chased by the spectre of outsourcing immediately after graduating college.

So yeah, I'm currently a product designer, and I love the product I design. I'm happy to say the folks that use it love it too.

What do you think about all of this AI stuff? ▼
GenAI's encroachment on the creative process has not been comfortable.

I see the ire and desire to be compensated for our creativity extracted as training data. I believe in the data dignity model proposed by Jaron Lanier. If our creative contributions are accessed when generating an output, we deserve payment.

We should be incentivized to contribute in a way that directly benefits others.

How do you balance your own creativity with AI? ▼
I've heard it said that the internet will soon be awash in B+ content thanks to Gen AI. So these tools allow everyone to create content that anyone would consider good enough. But there will always be a huge chasm between good and great - meaningless and meaningful - inert and provocative.

That's where artistry can be found - attempting to bridge those gaps.

Will AI ever cross the chasm from "good enough" to "great" on its own? ▼
That chasm is built on discernment. I don't doubt that AI could develop that type of taste. But AI sits indifferently, waiting. Requiring the initiation, the spark, the cohesion that comes with the drive of an idea.

The human pursuit feels missing in AI right now. There's no desire, no direction, no discernment in AI. It's a wonderful tool and offers an increasing level of fidelity to realize our ideas. But when AI is given open pastures to create, that's when reductive "slop" tends to prevail.
If Jaron Lanier's data dignity model existed tomorrow, what would change first? ▼
I would hope the enthusiasm to participate and contribute our perspectives to the collective whole. I wish we all were able to sustain our lives on the simple act of expressing our perspectives. What a wonderful world we'd have if our sense of worth was directly tied to our sense of self. A fella can dream.

What is your process? ▼
It's never really the same thing twice.

I often start with a draft, something tangible, if not a little disjointed. Then I'll load that draft as context into a conversation with the latest, greatest AI chat interface. Then I bounce around in discussion over the core nugget in my draft.

Often, I'll request a revised draft after that conversation. From there I edit, tweak, adjust, add imagery and upload.
Is there a step you've tried to skip that always bites you? ▼
Well, kinda, early on I tried the hacky "write 1500 words on this concept, make it sound like so and so, make it longer, make it funnier, no, like, dry funny." That type of prompt engineering just fizzles at scale. This is a marathon, not a sprint. I'm not looking for a quick blast of click. I've got stuff to say and avenues to explore.
How do you know when the conversation is "done" and it's time to write? ▼
Well, it generally starts with a draft of some kind, just a dump of thoughts from the week. That's the contextual foundation for the conversation that helps find the spine of my thoughts, then we build the skeletal structure of a piece, flesh it out, bat it around, edit, edit, edit, and I like to try and push it out before noon. So it's equal parts feeling its ready and wanting it done.
